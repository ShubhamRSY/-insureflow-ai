Zero Token Architecture

Ordinary software first. AI last. You can measure it.

Most AI underwriting tools send every page to a language model (and pay for it). Rytera asks: can ordinary rules or a trained scorer do this? Most of underwriting can. So you get faster, cheaper, repeatable answers. When a language model is truly needed, the cost is counted and shown on the job. Every word, said simply.

Layer 1

Ordinary rules

Readers, matching numbers, COPE, your rate engine, and compliance rules do everything they can. No AI bill. Same answer twice.

0 AI cost · fully repeatable
Layer 2

Trained scorers

Eight models score loss, fraud, churn, premium, book risk, and default — like a calculator, not a chatbot. No prompt. Same inputs, same output.

0 AI cost · repeatable
Layer 3

A language model — only when needed

Judgment that genuinely needs reading and writing, with a budget per job. Never used to invent a fact code already knows.

Security & paper trail

Every decision defensible

Locked files, who can see what, and a pack an examiner can read are built in — not bolted on after.

Files stored locked

Each company’s work stays in its own lockbox. Social Security numbers, tax IDs, and dates of birth come off before any AI sees the page.

Who can see what

Only the right people in your company can open a file. Roles are written down. One company cannot see another’s jobs.

A pack for the examiner

Every decision, every change you made, every outside data check ships in a sealed zip. Nobody can quietly rewrite the record.

A person stays in charge

A licensed underwriter signs. Who can sign what size of risk is written down. AI proposes. You dispose. Every change is recorded.

State regulatory engine

State law on every quote — not a footnote

Rytera ships a compliance engine with 50 states + DC, line-specific rules for auto, property, liability, workers’ comp, life, health, cyber, marine, and more. Rate filing method, surplus-lines diligence, tort model, binder rules, and prompt-pay deadlines are checked before a memo goes bind-ready. Regulatory feeds from state DOIs, NAIC, and the Federal Register are polled and surfaced for staff review.

California CDI

  • Prior-approval vs use-and-file by line
  • Earthquake & wildfire disclosure rules
  • Prop 103 rate-intervention context on filings

Texas TDI

  • Windstorm & coastal pool routing
  • Surplus-lines tax & stamping requirements
  • Prompt-pay on claims (statutory days)

New York DFS

  • Prior approval on many personal lines
  • Free-look & cancellation notice periods
  • Surplus-lines diligent-search documentation

Florida OIR

  • Assignment-of-benefits fraud flags

  • NCCI vs independent bureau class codes
  • Experience mod & payroll audit hooks
