Zero Token Architecture

Ordinary software first. AI last. You can measure it.

Most AI underwriting tools send every page to a language model (and pay for it). Rytera asks: can ordinary rules or a trained scorer do this? Most of underwriting can. So you get faster, cheaper, repeatable answers. When a language model is truly needed, the cost is counted and shown on the job. Every word, said simply.

A quiet promise

Named insureds never leave the gate.

Named insured means the person or company on the policy. PII means private details like Social Security numbers. Before any AI (a language model) looks at a file, we remove those. Your underwriter still sees the real file. The AI does not. Their names never leave the gate. Same promise on insurance, mortgage, and lending.

In plain English

Every technical word on this site, said simply.

If a page used a desk word, this is what it means. No one should need a glossary from another company to read ours.

Underwriter
The licensed person who decides yes, no, or not yet on a risk. Rytera drafts. They still sign.
Carrier / MGA
Carrier = the insurance company that takes the risk. MGA = a specialist team allowed to underwrite on a carrier’s behalf.
Submission
The pile a broker sends when they want a quote: PDFs, spreadsheets, emails, photos.
The memo (bind-ready)
A clear recommendation you can read and sign. Bind means issue the policy. Ready means you could, after you sign — not a pile of notes you still have to rewrite.
Practice mode (shadow)
Run Rytera on real files without issuing a policy. Prove it on your book. Go live when you say so.
Appetite
What your company is willing to write. Files that don’t fit get referred out before they eat a day.
Triage
Sorting the queue so the files that need a human rise first, and the obvious no’s don’t steal the morning.
Loss run / SOV / ACORD
Loss run = history of claims. SOV (schedule of values) = the list of buildings and what they’re worth. ACORD = a standard insurance form brokers already use.
Limit, deductible, exposure
Limit = the most the policy would pay. Deductible = what the customer pays first. Exposure = how much is actually at risk.
COPE
Construction, Occupancy, Protection, Exposure — the four things a property underwriter always checks (how it’s built, who uses it, fire protection, what sits next door).
Oracles (CLUE, NCCI, A+, CAT)
Outside data checks: prior claims, workers-comp history, catastrophe risk. We only treat them as real when your accounts are connected. We never fake a clean history.
Rate book / SERFF / filing
Your official prices, as filed with the state. SERFF is the system states use to receive those filings. We will not quote off a demo book and call it yours.
Policy admin / Guidewire / PAS
The system that actually issues the policy. Bind without re-key means the quote lands there in full — you should not have to type it again.
IMAP, S3, SFTP
How files already arrive: email (IMAP), a cloud folder (S3), or a secure drop (SFTP). We meet the file where it lives.
AI / LLM
A language model — software that can read and write. We use it only where judgment is needed. It never issues a policy. Names come off first.
Zero Token Architecture
Most steps are ordinary rules and checks (no AI bill). AI is the last resort, counted, and never used to invent a fact.
Human-in-the-loop
A person stays in charge. Software proposes. A licensed underwriter disposes. Every change they make is recorded.
Paper trail / exam pack
A sealed record of what was read, checked, and signed. When a regulator asks why this file, you hand them that — not a story from three shared drives.
PII / de-identification
Private details: names, Social Security numbers, tax IDs, dates of birth. Stripping them before AI sees the page is de-identification.
Catalog vs live
Catalog = we show the product, but we will not pretend we can price or bind it yet. Live = your real rates and connections are in, so a quote is honest.
Line desk vs staff desk
Line underwriters work the files in the branch. Staff underwriters at home office set the rules the line desk follows.
Filing-grade
Priced from your official, state-filed rates — not a demo book, not a guess.
Tokens
The unit an AI vendor bills. “Zero token” means that step used ordinary software, so there is no AI bill and the answer is repeatable.
Locked files / who can see what
Fernet = files stored locked. JWT / RBAC = only the right people in your company can open them. SHA-256 = a seal so nobody can quietly change the record.
Fail-closed
If a real data feed is missing, we stop or refer the file. We do not invent a clean history so the screen looks pretty.
Re-key
Typing the same quote into another system by hand. Bind without re-key means the policy system receives the full quote.
Subjectivities
Conditions that must be true before the policy can go live (an inspection, a missing form, a signed warranty).
Authority matrix
Who is allowed to sign what size of risk. A junior underwriter cannot silently bind a jumbo account.
IVANS / SharePoint / Drive
Industry mailboxes and cloud folders where files already live. We connect when you contract them; until then we do not pretend they are live.
GL, WC, D&O, E&O
General liability, workers’ compensation, directors & officers, errors & omissions — common commercial covers. We say the long name first.
UL, OPD, CI, UBI
Universal life, outpatient (day-to-day doctor visits), critical illness, usage-based insurance (price from how you drive). Catalog until we can honestly price them.
ISO / AAIS / NCCI
Industry groups that publish standard rates and class codes. Carriers start there, then add their own expenses and profit.
E&S (excess & surplus)
Risks the regular market will not write. A specialist market can, with extra checks on who is allowed to bind.
TRID, Reg Z, HMDA / ECOA, Reg B
Mortgage and lending fairness rules: clear closing costs, honest credit pricing, equal treatment, and a written reason if we say no.
MVR / CLUE / HO-3
MVR = driving record. CLUE = prior home/auto claims. HO-3 = a common homeowners policy form.
Replacement cost
What it would cost to rebuild, not what the building would sell for. A small house cannot claim a warehouse rebuild number.
Cross-field check
Two facts on the same file have to be able to be true together. Payroll needs people. A license cannot be issued after the policy starts.
EXIF / ELA
EXIF = the camera tag on a photo (who saved it, when). ELA = JPEG error-level analysis — a local paste often leaves a hotter recompress scar than an original shot.
Fraud ring / graph net
Files linked by the same phone, address, tax ID, or IP. A small neural net on that graph scores whether the cluster looks like a ring — not a guess from a single file.
Telematics / cyber scan
Telematics = what the car actually did (miles, hard brakes). Cyber scan = an outside look at a domain. We compare those to the questionnaire only when the feed is live.
Citation gate
A critical number without a page, box, or source ref is not a fact. It fails straight-through processing and stays off the bind-ready memo until grounded.
Self-RAG / HyDE
Self-RAG = retrieve, ask if the context is enough, retrieve again if not. HyDE = search with a hypothetical guideline paragraph when the desk question is too short for vector match.
Glass box
Click a value, see the page highlight. Warm color means low confidence. Approve still needs a licensed person.
Zero-hallucination gate
Target: zero uncited money, limits, or totals on a bind-ready memo. Anything invented is stripped and the file is referred. We do not rubber-stamp a pretty number.
